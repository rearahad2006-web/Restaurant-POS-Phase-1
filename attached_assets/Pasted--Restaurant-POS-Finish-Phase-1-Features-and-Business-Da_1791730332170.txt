# Restaurant POS — Finish Phase 1 Features and Business-Day Management

Work on the existing Restaurant POS project. Do not rebuild it or start Phase 2.

## Critical rules
- Preserve existing POS features, reports, Excel export, orders, payments, expenses, menu, tables, staff, settings, and saved data.
- Reuse existing components, storage/repository functions, permissions, and printing utilities.
- Make small, targeted changes.
- Do not add SQLite, cloud services, desktop packaging, or direct/silent thermal printing.
- Do not claim tests passed unless actually run.
- If the dependency installation is blocked by Replit's package firewall, stop and report the exact blocker. Do not bypass the firewall or damage the project.

## Feature 1 — Deals and Combo Offers

- Add a manager-only Deals page.
- Managers can create, edit, activate, deactivate, and delete deals.
- Each deal includes a name, optional description, existing menu items, quantities, and a fixed price in PKR.
- Cashiers can select active deals in the POS.
- Charge the fixed deal price without charging the included items again.
- Separately ordered menu items must still be charged normally.
- Support deal quantity changes and removal.
- Save a snapshot of the deal in each completed order so later edits do not change historical receipts or reports.
- Enforce existing manager/cashier permissions.

## Feature 2 — Separate Kitchen Order Tokens

Add a **Print Kitchen Token** action to existing orders.

Include restaurant name/logo, order number, date/time, order type, table number, relevant customer/delivery details, item names and quantities, deal contents, and preparation notes.

- Do not show prices, totals, or payment details.
- Keep the kitchen token separate from the customer receipt.
- Use the existing browser print approach.
- Print the selected order only and trigger printing once per action.
- Do not implement silent printing or a full kitchen display system.

## Feature 3 — Manual Quantity Entry

- Let cashiers click a cart quantity and type a positive whole number.
- Apply the new quantity with Enter or when leaving the field.
- Support regular items and deals for Dine-in, Takeaway, and Delivery.
- Keep plus, minus, and remove controls working.
- Recalculate all applicable totals, discounts, and delivery charges.
- Reject zero, negative, decimal, and invalid quantities.
- Allow the field to be temporarily empty during editing without removing the item.
- Restore the previous quantity if invalid input is committed.
- Pressing Enter in the quantity field must not accidentally complete the order.
- Never modify completed orders.

## Feature 4 — Automatic Dine-in Table Selection

- When Dine-in is selected, automatically select the first available configured table.
- For the next new Dine-in order, select the next available table in a sensible sequence.
- Skip tables occupied by active orders.
- Show the selected table clearly and let the cashier change it manually.
- Switching to Takeaway or Delivery must clear the current Dine-in selection.
- Switching back to Dine-in should select an available table.
- If no tables are available, show a clear message; never assign an occupied table.
- Use existing table records and status logic. Do not create duplicate tables.
- Do not permanently mark a table occupied merely because it was selected in an unfinished cart.
- Save the correct table with the order and show it on receipts and kitchen tokens.
- Preserve existing order history and table-management behavior.

## Feature 5 — Restaurant Business Day: Start Day and End Day

The restaurant operates from **8:00 AM until approximately 3:00 AM the following calendar day**. A business day is not the same as a calendar day.

### A. Start Day

- Add a clear **Start Day** action to the existing POS/manager interface.
- A new business day normally starts at 8:00 AM.
- Record the business-day ID, business date, actual start timestamp, starting user, and opening cash amount if the existing payment/cash workflow supports it.
- Prevent accidentally starting multiple business days simultaneously.
- If the business day is already open, show its status and allow authorized users to continue working.
- If the restaurant starts late or closes late, do not lose or misclassify orders.

### B. Business-day date rules

- Orders made from 8:00 AM through 11:59 PM belong to that day's business day.
- Orders made after midnight and before 3:00 AM belong to the business day that started at 8:00 AM the previous calendar day.
- For example, an order at 1:30 AM on October 12 belongs to the business day that started at 8:00 AM on October 11, provided that business day is still active.
- Use a consistent local restaurant timezone and store reliable timestamps.
- Daily reports, payments, expenses, and sales totals must use the business-day assignment rather than grouping everything by calendar date.
- Preserve historical data and existing report/Excel functionality.

### C. End Day

- Add a clear **End Day** button for authorized staff.
- Before closing, show a summary of orders, sales, payment methods, expenses, cancellations, and cash totals where available.
- Require confirmation before closing.
- Record the end timestamp, closing user, and closing cash amount if supported.
- Once closed, prevent new orders from being assigned to that business day.
- Preserve all orders and records. Never delete or reset sales data.
- A new business day should be started explicitly; do not silently create duplicate days.

### D. Logout, power loss, and recovery

- On logout, ask the user whether to end the business day or leave it open, according to their permissions. Make the consequence clear.
- If the computer loses power or the browser closes unexpectedly, preserve all saved orders and the open business-day record.
- On the next launch, detect an interrupted session and clearly show whether the previous business day is still open.
- Let an authorized manager resume the existing day or close it with a recorded timestamp and confirmation.
- Do not pretend the application can detect the exact power-off time if it was not recorded.
- Do not lose or duplicate orders during recovery.
- Prevent cashiers from performing manager-only closing actions if existing roles require manager authorization.
- If an unfinished cart cannot be safely recovered, explain this to the user rather than silently creating an order.
